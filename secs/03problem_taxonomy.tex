\section{Problem Formulation and Taxonomy}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{From Records to a Trainable Distribution}

Let a raw episode be
\begin{equation}
e=(m,\,o_{1:T},\,s_{1:T},\,a_{1:T},\,\ell,\,y),
\end{equation}
where $m$ stores provenance, embodiment, sensor, calibration, operator, and timing
metadata; $o$, $s$, and $a$ are observations, states, and actions; $\ell$ denotes
available language or task context; and $y$ contains outcomes or interventions.
Fields may be missing, weakly synchronized, differently parameterized, or
incorrect.  Human video may lack $s$ and robot $a$ entirely, while generated data
may contain all fields but violate geometry or dynamics.

A preparation pipeline is a composition of operators
\begin{equation}
\pipeline_{c}=p_K\circ\cdots\circ p_2\circ p_1,
\qquad
\pipeline_c(\rawdata)=\traindata,
\end{equation}
conditioned on context $c=(f,b,\tau,k,B)$: learner family $f$, target embodiment
$b$, task/deployment distribution $\tau$, training stage $k$, and resource budget
$B$.  Operators need not preserve episode boundaries; they may emit segments,
transitions, counterexamples, paired modalities, or synthetic branches.  Each
prepared unit should retain
\begin{equation}
z_j=(x_j,t_j,w_j,r_j,g_j,v_j),
\end{equation}
with input $x_j$, target $t_j$, sampling weight $w_j$, training role $r_j$, lineage
$g_j$, and version $v_j$.  Roles distinguish, for example, broad pretraining,
embodiment alignment, task adaptation, recovery, negative/safety supervision, and
verifier calibration.  Lineage makes every label, coordinate transform, filter,
and generator traceable to the raw record.

The desired pipeline maximizes deployment utility rather than a standalone
cleanliness score:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta_{\pipeline}&=\operatorname{Train}
(f,\pipeline_c(\rawdata),B_{\mathrm{train}}),\\
\pipeline^{*}&=\underset{\pipeline\in\Pi}{\arg\max}\;
\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\utility(\theta_{\pipeline})]\\[-0.2em]
&\quad-\lambda C_{\mathrm{prep}}-\mu C_{\mathrm{train}}-\nu R.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:prep-objective}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathrm{prep}}$ includes human, model, simulator, storage, and robot costs,
and $R$ covers leakage, safety, bias, and feedback-instability risk.  This
formulation exposes a common evaluation mistake: keeping the number of epochs
fixed after filtering changes both the number of optimizer steps and the number
of times each retained example is observed.  The claimed intervention is then
confounded with compute and resampling.

\subsection{Quality Is a Conditional Vector}

An episode has no policy-independent scalar ``quality.''  We instead represent
observable properties as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\qualityvec(e)=[&q_{\mathrm{tech}},q_{\mathrm{semantic}},q_{\mathrm{control}},
q_{\mathrm{physical}},\\
&q_{\mathrm{coverage}},q_{\mathrm{alignment}},q_{\mathrm{safety}}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and reserve \emph{value} for its conditional marginal contribution:
\begin{equation}
V(e\mid c,\mathcal{D})=
\mathbb{E}[\utility(\theta_{\mathcal{D}\cup e})-
\utility(\theta_{\mathcal{D}})].
\label{eq:conditional-value}
\end{equation}
The value depends on the surrounding mixture because an individually valid
episode can be redundant, restore missing coverage, or conflict with an action
convention.  It also depends on the learner: RoboMimic's controlled study showed
strong interactions among demonstration quality, observation, algorithm, and
training choices~\cite{mandlekar2021what_2108_03298}; formal work identifies
action divergence and transition diversity as coupled determinants of imitation
performance~\cite{belkhale2023data_2306_02437}.  Equations~\ref{eq:prep-objective}
and~\ref{eq:conditional-value} are conceptual---exact marginal retraining is
usually infeasible---but they define what proxy metrics are trying to estimate.

\subsection{Four Orthogonal Taxonomy Axes}

Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy-axes} summarizes the taxonomy.  Its axes are orthogonal
questions, not a partition of papers.

\input{tables/taxonomy_axes}

\textbf{Operation.}  We distinguish acquisition/task design, technical
validation and repair, representation/alignment, segmentation/relabeling,
scoring/filtering, weighting/routing, augmentation/synthesis, and packaging or
versioning.  Filtering changes membership; weighting changes sampling
probability; routing changes the training stage or objective.  Treating all three
as ``curation'' hides materially different interventions.

\textbf{Decision signal.}  Signals progress from inexpensive rules and human
labels through pretrained models and representation statistics to learner-aware
loss, influence, simulator feedback, and real execution.  This is not a simple
quality hierarchy.  Later signals are more target-relevant but costlier and can
be noisier; a cheap checksum is the right detector for a corrupted file, while a
real rollout is excessive.  A mature pipeline cascades signals, reserving costly
evidence for ambiguous or high-impact decisions.

\textbf{Utility evidence.}  Intrinsic metrics show that an intended property
changed.  Proxy metrics show agreement with a verifier or estimator.  Controlled
retraining tests learning utility.  Deployment tests whether utility survives
physical and distribution shift.  These answer different questions and should
not be merged into a single score.

\textbf{Lifecycle and authority.}  Offline preparation freezes $\pipeline$
before training.  Training-time methods update sampling from losses or progress;
post-training methods mine failures; continual methods change acquisition and
verification after deployment.  A further distinction asks whether people
predefine that loop or an agent chooses among preparation operators and
diagnostic experiments.  The axis thus separates a static synthetic dataset, a
data engine that uses policy failures to trigger a prescribed generator, and an
agent that can decide to collect, relabel, audit, abstain, or roll back---even if
all use the word ``augmentation.''

\subsection{Lifecycle Backbone and Secondary Attributes}

For navigation, we arrange operations along eight recurring stages: (1) source
and task design; (2) ingestion and technical quality control; (3) standardization
and cross-embodiment alignment; (4) segmentation and semantic enrichment; (5)
scoring, filtering, weighting, and routing; (6) augmentation and synthesis; (7)
layered validation; and (8) training/deployment feedback.  The order is not
strict.  A failed generated trajectory can return to segmentation; deployment
can revise source design; and the same verifier may act before and after training.

We additionally code the transformed object (video/frame, state/sensor, action,
language, episode/segment/transition, cross-modal link, or task/source
distribution) and objective (correctness, diversity, coverage, difficulty,
executability, physical consistency, generalization, efficiency, stability, or
safety).  These attributes reveal underexplored combinations.  Current work is
dense in offline episode filtering guided by representations and in visual
synthesis checked by semantic similarity.  It is sparse in transition-level
cross-embodiment value, deployment-calibrated validation of generated contact,
and cost-aware routing of failures among discard, relabel, recovery training, and
new acquisition.  Almost no system jointly learns operator choice, experiment
allocation, promotion, stopping, and rollback.  These gaps motivate
Sections~\ref{sec:recipes-engines} and~\ref{sec:agenda}.
