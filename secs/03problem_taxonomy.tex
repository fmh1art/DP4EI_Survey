\section{Problem Formulation and Taxonomy}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{From Records to a Trainable Distribution}

Let $T$ denote the episode length.  For a raw episode $e$, let $m$ contain its
provenance, embodiment, sensor, calibration, operator, and timing metadata.  Let
$o_{1:T}$, $s_{1:T}$, and $a_{1:T}$ denote observations, states, and actions.
The variables $\ell$ and $y$ denote language or task context and recorded outcomes
or interventions.  We write the episode as
\begin{equation}
e=(m,\,o_{1:T},\,s_{1:T},\,a_{1:T},\,\ell,\,y).
\end{equation}
Fields may be missing, weakly synchronized, differently parameterized, or
incorrect.  Human video may lack $s$ and robot $a$ entirely, while generated data
may contain all fields but violate geometry or dynamics.

Let $\rawdata$ be a collection of raw episodes and $\traindata$ the resulting
training units.  A preparation pipeline contains $K$ operators
$p_1,\ldots,p_K$.  It is conditioned on a context
$c=(f,b,\tau,k,B)$, where $f$ is the learner family, $b$ the target embodiment,
$\tau$ the task and deployment distribution, $k$ the training stage, and $B$ the
available resource budget.  The pipeline is
\begin{equation}
\pipeline_{c}=p_K\circ\cdots\circ p_2\circ p_1,
\qquad
\pipeline_c(\rawdata)=\traindata.
\end{equation}
Operators need not preserve episode boundaries.  They may emit segments,
transitions, counterexamples, paired modalities, or synthetic branches.

For each prepared unit $z_j$, let $x_j$ and $t_j$ be its input and target.  Let
$w_j$ be its sampling weight, $r_j$ its training role, $g_j$ its lineage record,
and $v_j$ its version.  The stored unit is
\begin{equation}
z_j=(x_j,t_j,w_j,r_j,g_j,v_j).
\end{equation}
Roles distinguish, for example, broad pretraining,
embodiment alignment, task adaptation, recovery, negative examples, safety
supervision, and verifier calibration.  Lineage makes every label, coordinate transform, filter,
and generator traceable to the raw record.

For a candidate pipeline $\pipeline$ from the set $\Pi$, training with budget
$B_{\mathrm{train}}$ yields the policy
$\theta_{\pipeline}=\operatorname{Train}(f,\pipeline_c(\rawdata),
B_{\mathrm{train}})$.  Let $\utility(\theta)$ denote expected policy utility on
$\tau$.  The terms $C_{\mathrm{prep}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{train}}$ denote preparation
and training cost, and $R$ denotes leakage, safety, bias, and feedback risk.  With
non-negative trade-off weights $\lambda$, $\mu$, and $\nu$, the desired pipeline
maximizes deployment utility rather than a standalone cleanliness score:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\pipeline^{*}=\underset{\pipeline\in\Pi}{\arg\max}\quad
&\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\utility(\theta_{\pipeline})]\\[-0.2em]
&-\lambda C_{\mathrm{prep}}-\mu C_{\mathrm{train}}-\nu R.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:prep-objective}
\end{equation}
Preparation cost includes human review, model inference, simulation, storage,
and robot time.  This objective exposes a common evaluation mistake.  Keeping
the number of epochs fixed after filtering changes both the number of optimizer steps and the number
of times each retained example is observed.  The claimed intervention is then
confounded with compute and resampling.

\subsection{Quality Is Conditional and Multidimensional}

An episode has no policy-independent scalar ``quality.''  We record technical,
semantic, control, physical, coverage, alignment, and safety properties
separately.  These observable properties are not themselves training value.
For a prepared dataset $\mathcal{D}$ and context $c$, let
$\theta_{\mathcal{D}}$ denote the policy trained on that dataset.  Let $Z(e;c)$
be the prepared training units derived from a candidate raw episode $e$ under
context $c$.  We use \emph{value} for the expected marginal contribution of that
episode:
\begin{equation}
V(e\mid c,\mathcal{D})=
\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\utility(\theta_{\mathcal{D}\cup Z(e;c)})-
\utility(\theta_{\mathcal{D}})].
\label{eq:conditional-value}
\end{equation}
The value depends on the surrounding mixture because an individually valid
episode can be redundant, restore missing coverage, or conflict with an action
convention.  It also depends on the learner: RoboMimic's controlled study showed
strong interactions among demonstration quality, observation, algorithm, and
training choices~\cite{mandlekar2021what_2108_03298}.  Formal work identifies
action divergence and transition diversity as coupled determinants of imitation
performance~\cite{belkhale2023data_2306_02437}.  Equations~\ref{eq:prep-objective}
and~\ref{eq:conditional-value} are conceptual.  Exact marginal retraining is
usually infeasible, but these objectives state what proxy metrics are trying to
estimate.

\input{tables/taxonomy_axes}

The operation axis distinguishes acquisition and task design, technical
validation and repair, representation and alignment, segmentation and relabeling,
scoring and filtering, weighting and routing, augmentation and synthesis, and packaging or
versioning.  Filtering changes membership.  Weighting changes sampling
probability, while routing changes the training stage or objective.  Treating all
three
as ``curation'' hides materially different interventions.

The decision-signal axis ranges from inexpensive rules and human
labels through pretrained models and representation statistics to feedback from
the target learner, simulator, or real execution.  We call a signal
\emph{learner-aware} when it depends on the losses, predictions, or outcomes of
the learner being trained.  This is not a simple quality hierarchy.  Later
signals are more target-relevant but costlier and can
be noisier.  A cheap checksum is the right detector for a corrupted file, while a
real rollout is excessive.  A mature pipeline applies signals in stages and reserves costly
evidence for ambiguous or high-impact decisions.

The utility-evidence axis distinguishes what a reported result establishes.
Intrinsic metrics show that an intended property changed.  Proxy metrics show
agreement with a verifier or estimator.  Controlled
retraining tests learning utility.  Deployment tests whether utility survives
physical and distribution shift.  These answer different questions and should
not be merged into a single score.

The lifecycle axis records when the data decision changes and who controls it.
Offline preparation freezes $\pipeline$ before training.  Training-time methods
update sampling from losses or progress.
Post-training methods mine failures, while continual methods change acquisition and
verification after deployment.  A further distinction asks whether people
predefine that loop or an agent chooses among preparation operators and
diagnostic experiments.  The axis separates a static synthetic dataset, a
data engine that uses policy failures to trigger a prescribed generator, and an
agent that can decide to collect, relabel, audit, abstain, or roll back, even if
all use the word ``augmentation.''

\subsection{Lifecycle Backbone and Secondary Attributes}

For navigation, we arrange operations along eight recurring stages.  The first
four cover source and task design, ingestion and technical quality control,
cross-embodiment alignment, and semantic enrichment.  The next three cover
selection and routing, augmentation and synthesis, and layered validation.  The
last stage uses training or deployment feedback to revise earlier decisions.
The order is not
strict.  A failed generated trajectory can return to segmentation.  Deployment
can revise source design, and the same verifier may act before and after training.

We additionally code what is transformed and at what granularity.  The object may
be a video, frame, state, sensor stream, action, language instruction, episode,
segment, transition, cross-modal link, or task and source distribution.  We
separately record whether the objective concerns correctness, diversity,
coverage, difficulty, executability, physical consistency, generalization,
efficiency, stability, or safety.  These attributes reveal underexplored
combinations.  Most current work concentrates on representation-guided episode
filtering and visual synthesis checked by semantic similarity.  Fewer studies
examine transition-level value across embodiments, calibrate generated contact
against deployment evidence, or route failures by cost among discard,
relabeling, recovery training, and new acquisition.  Almost no system jointly
learns which operator to run, how to allocate experiments, when to promote or
roll back a version, and when to stop.  These gaps motivate
Sections~\ref{sec:recipes-engines} and~\ref{sec:agenda}.
