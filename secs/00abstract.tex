Embodied foundation models are trained on expanding mixtures of robot
demonstrations, human video, simulation, generated trajectories, and deployment
experience.  These records are not interchangeable: they differ in embodiment,
control semantics, timing, annotation, physical validity, and relevance to the
learner.  Raw scale is therefore an inadequate account of the data bottleneck.
This survey studies \emph{data preparation for embodied intelligence}: the
decisions that transform heterogeneous interactions into a trainable
distribution with verifiable downstream value.  We synthesize an auditable
evidence map of 211 papers and system reports through four orthogonal lenses:
the operation performed, its decision signal, the evidence used to establish
utility, and its lifecycle position.  This view unifies source design, technical
validation, cross-embodiment alignment, semantic enrichment, filtering and
mixture optimization, augmentation and synthesis, layered verification, and
failure-driven evolution.  We further reconstruct public recipes from
Qwen-RobotManip/RobotWorld, the $\pi$ family, GR00T, and Gemini Robotics, and
compare data engines by gap signal, data action, learner update, and stability
control rather than by incomparable end-to-end scores.  The evidence supports
six conclusions: quality is learner-, task-, stage-, and budget-conditional;
useful decisions increasingly occur below the episode; alignment is a
precondition for cross-source scale; training role is often more informative
than source type; generation and self-training move the bottleneck to independent
verification; and failures become useful only after typed annotation and
routing.  We distinguish intrinsic, proxy, controlled-training, and deployment
evidence, derive mechanism-level lessons from LLM data engineering, and formalize
an evolution from static curation to learner-aware selection, failure-driven
post-training, multi-round data engines, and ultimately auditable autonomous
data-preparation agents.  The resulting agenda targets utility-calibrated
metrics, cross-embodiment semantics, stable self-evolution, causal promotion
tests, rollback, and cost/safety-aware agent evaluation.
