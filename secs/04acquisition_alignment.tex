\section{Source Design, Ingestion, and Alignment}
\label{sec:acquisition}

Preparation starts when a collection process decides which behavior to record and how to record it.
The interface determines which states can be visited, how accurately actions are represented, which failures remain observable, and how much alignment will be required later.
We include these choices as source design when they change data compatibility or coverage.
The mechanical design of the collection hardware remains outside our scope.

\subsection{Sources Have Complementary Training Roles}

Real-robot teleoperation offers the strongest observation--action--physics alignment but is expensive and biased by the available robot, operator, and task script.
DROID reduces site bias through a common mobile manipulator and protocol deployed across 18 institutions, yielding roughly 76,000 demonstrations in diverse scenes~\cite{khazatsky2024droid_2403_12945}.
BridgeData V2 emphasizes an open, multi-task collection on a consistent platform~\cite{walke2023bridgedata_2308_12952}.
Open X-Embodiment takes the opposite integration route: convert existing datasets into a common interface and train across institutions and robots ~\cite{collaboration2023open_2310_08864}.
The former controls acquisition while the latter accepts heterogeneity and moves more alignment work after collection.
RoboTurk decouples demonstrators from the robot through remote crowdsourcing, making operator latency and identity preparation metadata rather than incidental noise~\cite{mandlekar2018roboturk_1811_02790}.
AXIS further integrates community collection, task generation, filtering, smoothing, and augmentation as an expanding system~\cite{zhao2026axis_2607_21588}.
As a recent preprint, it is evidence of an emerging system pattern rather than a mature standard.

Portable human-operated interfaces reduce the amount of target-robot time needed for collection.
UMI reproduces relevant gripper geometry, recovers six-degree-of-freedom motion, and reduces observation mismatch at deployment~\cite{chi2024universal_2402_10329}.
Human videos offer much greater semantic and environmental breadth, but usually lack robot states, metric scale, actions, forces, and reliable task boundaries.
Their use spans representation pretraining, observation transfer, and action reconstruction~\cite{ma2026robot_2604_27621}.
EgoMimic co-trains aligned egocentric human and robot demonstrations, whereas Hand-Object Interaction Pretraining (HOP) reconstructs three-dimensional hand--object interaction and retargets it into robot experience ~\cite{kareer2024egomimic_2410_24221,singh2024hand_2409_08273}.
Both illustrate that ``human video'' becomes action supervision only through explicit geometric and embodiment assumptions.
Simulation offers exact state and controlled generation of rare cases but inherits asset, rendering, dynamics, and contact mismatch.
Autonomous execution, as in AutoRT, produces records from the current policy in the actual environment.
It directly exposes parts of the task distribution the policy handles poorly but also concentrates data around its existing competence ~\cite{ahn2024autort_2401_12963}.

No source dominates along correctness, diversity, scale, and target alignment.
It is more useful to ask which training role a source can support.
Web and human video provide broad visual and task semantics.
Portable human demonstrations provide motion and temporal structure, while multi-robot corpora expose shared visual and action regularities.
Target-robot teleoperation calibrates control and contact.
Successful demonstrations can support task adaptation, while failed attempts and recoveries can support post-training and safety.
Assigning data to these distinct roles avoids two errors.
It prevents broad but weakly aligned data from being discarded when they still provide useful visual or semantic supervision.
It also prevents those data from silently serving as precise action targets for the target robot.

\subsection{Coverage Must Be Designed, Not Counted}

Episode count hides variation in task concepts, objects, backgrounds, viewpoints, initial conditions, operators, embodiments, outcomes, and transition density.
Repeating a narrow configuration can improve statistical precision without expanding coverage.
Adding a new environment can expand coverage but may also introduce a different action convention.
The acquisition target should be a structured coverage record rather than a global quota.
A practical collection system tracks cells such as $(\text{skill},\text{object},\text{scene}, \text{embodiment},\text{outcome})$, uncertainty in each cell, and marginal cost.
Controlled real-robot scaling experiments similarly find that, after enough repetition, expanding object and environment diversity is more valuable than additional demonstrations of the same configuration in their task setting ~\cite{lin2024data_2410_18647}.
The fitted scaling law should not be transferred unchanged across learners or collection protocols.
Its value lies in varying object and environment diversity separately.

An active acquisition policy chooses what to collect next from current evidence.
It must balance expected training value, new coverage, risk, and collection cost.
Uncertainty alone is unsafe because it can select sensor corruption, infeasible tasks, or states from which the robot cannot recover safely.
Candidate tasks also need executability and safety checks.
The resulting record must be labeled well enough to support its intended training role.
Most current systems still rely on hand-designed task proposals and success filters rather than an estimate of which additional record would most improve training.

\subsection{Ingestion and Technical Quality Control}

Technical validity is the cheapest part of the evidence ladder and should be resolved before semantic scoring.
A minimum ingestion contract verifies file readability, schema and version compatibility, units, tensor shapes, and required fields.
It checks clocks, dropped frames, cross-stream synchronization, and command-to-actuation delay.
Calibration, robot description, joint limits, reset boundaries, and controller identity must also be present.
Sensor checks cover blur, exposure, occlusion, saturation, missing values, and implausible jumps.
Action checks cover range, idle fraction, clipping, jerk, collisions, and emergency interventions.

These checks should emit repair decisions and uncertainty, not only pass or fail.
Interpolation can repair short sensor gaps but may fabricate contact transitions.
Smoothing can remove teleoperation jitter but also erase deliberate high-frequency corrections.
The unmodified raw stream, repair parameters, and reason code must remain in lineage.
In contact-rich multimodal data such as RH20T, time alignment across vision, internal robot state, force and torque, audio, and touch is itself a semantic precondition, not mere storage hygiene~\cite{fang2023rh20t_2307_00595}.

\subsection{Four Layers of Cross-Embodiment Alignment}

Common schemas enable loading and batching, but shape compatibility does not imply supervision compatibility.
Cross-embodiment alignment has at least four layers.

Structural alignment maps fields, modalities, missing degrees of freedom, and gripper or hand state into a common schema.
Fixed action slots plus an embodiment mask make heterogeneity explicit, but padding only states that a degree of freedom is absent.

Spatial and control alignment specifies whether actions are joint positions, velocities, absolute or delta end-effector poses, forces, or discrete tokens.
It also records the reference frame, rotation convention, units, controller gains, and whether a command is a target or an executed state.
Converting to a camera- or end-effector-centric representation can remove base-placement variation, but calibration error then becomes label noise.
Some mappings are many-to-one: compressing a dexterous hand into ``gripper openness'' loses interaction semantics and is not invertible.

Temporal alignment reconciles sensor rate, controller rate, observation latency, action horizon, interpolation, and the meaning of an action chunk.
A frame paired with a command issued several control cycles later teaches the policy to associate the observation with the wrong action.
Resampling should not introduce frequency artifacts or average away contact boundaries.
Both command time and measured execution time should be retained when the delay varies.

Behavioral alignment asks whether the same normalized action has the same consequence under different morphology, kinematics, compliance, and controllers.
This is the least solved layer.
An embodiment-conditioned action encoder can incorporate kinematic graphs and control metadata.
Mapping an action to another embodiment and back can test whether its meaning is preserved, while a rollout can test whether the mapped action produces the intended state transition.
Open X-Embodiment demonstrates the benefit of a common training interface, but its heterogeneity also motivates learner-aware mixture methods such as Re-Mix~\cite{collaboration2023open_2310_08864,hejna2024re_2408_14037}.

\subsection{Jointly Transform All Coupled Modalities}

Any spatial or temporal transformation must act consistently on observations, states, actions, language, and success conditions.
Mirroring an image without mirroring lateral actions and the words ``left'' and ``right'' creates confident label error.
Changing camera pose requires transforming camera-frame actions.
Time reversal is valid only for a reversible segment and an appropriately reversed task description.
It is generally invalid for pouring, breaking, or frictional contact.
An augmentation is valid only when the observation, action, and task description preserve the same state transition.
Image plausibility alone is insufficient.

Preparation artifacts also need a data contract that records source and license, collection context, robot and controller, coordinate frames, clocks, transformations, labels, confidence, training roles, relationships between training and test records, and version hashes.
Without this contract, a later score, failure report, or model version cannot be traced to the preparation decision that produced the training example.
