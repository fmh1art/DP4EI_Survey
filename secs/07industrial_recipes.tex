\section{Industrial Data Recipes for Embodied Foundation Models}
\label{sec:industrial-recipes}

The preceding sections isolate individual preparation operations. Industrial embodied models instead disclose the data recipes defined in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}. These reports matter because they show which combinations can be operated at large scale. They do not provide a common experimental setting. Several parts of a recipe usually change together, and the raw data, mixture weights, or intermediate versions are often private. We therefore use end-to-end reports as evidence about disclosed system design and reserve causal claims for reported component controls.

Figure~\ref{fig:industrial-recipe-map} summarizes the common functional structure reconstructed from five public recipe families. It does not imply that every system implements the same sequence. The figure separates source roles from scale, and separates alignment from later validation. These distinctions are lost when a recipe is reduced to total hours or episode count.

\subsection{Sources Are Assigned Roles Before They Are Mixed}

The recipes agree that heterogeneous sources are not interchangeable. Qwen-RobotManip combines robot demonstrations, egocentric human video, and human-to-robot synthesis, but each source enters through a different preparation path~\cite{yuan2026qwen_2606_17846}. The $\pi$ family uses cross-robot data, web data, high-level supervision, target-task demonstrations, and later policy rollouts for different learning objectives~\cite{intelligence20250_2504_16054,intelligence20260_2604_15483}. GR00T similarly distinguishes broad web and human data, simulated or generated trajectories, and real robot demonstrations~\cite{nvidia2025gr00t_2503_14734}. Gemini Robotics combines multimodal pretraining data, demonstrations from several embodiments, and smaller specialization sets~\cite{team2025gemini_2503_20020,team2025gemini_2510_03342}. Qwen-RobotWorld retains both embodied video and general multimodal data while controlling their mixture during world-model training~\cite{zhang2026qwen_2606_17030}.

This role assignment resolves an apparent tension between breadth and action precision. Web or human video can provide objects, tasks, and temporal structure without serving as calibrated action targets. Cross-robot data can teach shared regularities while target-robot demonstrations ground control and contact. Generated data can expand task or scene coverage while audited real trajectories preserve embodiment fidelity. A source should therefore be described by the supervision it can support at a given stage, not by a global quality label.

The recipes also illustrate why aggregate scale is a poor comparison unit. Qwen-RobotManip reports approximately 38,100 hours across robot, human, and synthesized data, while Qwen-RobotWorld reports 8.6 million clips and more than 200 million frames~\cite{yuan2026qwen_2606_17846,zhang2026qwen_2606_17030}. These quantities measure different temporal resolutions and supervision. They cannot establish that one corpus contains more action learning signal than another. Comparable disclosure would report source-specific units, sampling exposure, training role, and the number of units that pass the checks required for that role.

\subsection{Alignment Determines Whether Scale Can Transfer}

The most concrete industrial contribution is often an alignment mechanism rather than a larger raw pool. Qwen-RobotManip separates representation, motion, and behavior alignment. Its masked 80-dimensional state--action schema fixes field meaning across morphologies. Camera-frame end-effector deltas reduce differences in base placement, while view and embodiment information remains visible to the model~\cite{yuan2026qwen_2606_17846}. The same pipeline applies state--action and cross-modal checks before mixing. Its audit reports strong source-specific rejection, including a subset for which a state--action trend check rejected 81\% of episodes. The result shows that a published trajectory can satisfy a storage format while remaining unsuitable for a shared action objective.

Gemini Robotics 1.5 calls its cross-embodiment alignment mechanism Motion Transfer. Its comparison among single-embodiment training, multi-embodiment training without this mechanism, and the full recipe isolates part of the value of action alignment~\cite{team2025gemini_2510_03342}. Other embodiments help most when the target embodiment has relatively little data. This asymmetry means that cross-embodiment results should be reported for each target rather than only as a pooled average.

GR00T addresses video without recorded controls in two ways~\cite{nvidia2025gr00t_2503_14734}. A latent-action model encodes motion in an internal action space, while an inverse-dynamics model estimates the action between observations. Neither estimate is a measured robot command. The uncertainty and intended role of the reconstructed supervision must remain explicit. The $\pi$ recipes instead emphasize normalization and conditioning on source, task, and quality metadata. Together, these systems expose three non-equivalent meanings of alignment: a common storage schema, a shared motion representation, and comparable behavioral consequences. Only the last supports direct transfer of action supervision.

\subsection{Routing Changes Across Training Stages}

Industrial mixtures are staged rather than fixed. $\pi_{0.5}$ uses broad pretraining for general representations and task structure, then post-trains on short successful target episodes while retaining selected broad supervision~\cite{intelligence20250_2504_16054}. $\pi_{0.7}$ further conditions on quality and strategy metadata so that lower-quality or alternative behavior need not be interpreted as the desired action target~\cite{intelligence20260_2604_15483}. Its reported ablations support a conditional claim. Mixed-quality data can help when metadata and the learning objective distinguish the desired behavior. The result does not imply that unmarked failures are safe imitation targets.

GR00T assigns generated, simulated, and real data to different stages. One reported post-training experiment uses equal sampling from real and generated trajectories, while a simulation branch retains successful trajectories~\cite{nvidia2025gr00t_2503_14734}. DreamGen adapts a video model to robot observations, generates task-conditioned videos, estimates actions from the generated motion, and then post-trains a policy~\cite{jang2025dreamgen_2505_12705}. Its instruction and physics diagnostics correlate with policy success in the reported experiments, but they do not certify each generated trajectory. The same GR00T report provides a useful retention result: omitting left-to-right handover data during later training removed that capability. Stage-specific routing therefore needs minimum exposure for rare or previously learned skills.

Qwen-RobotWorld applies a curriculum over view structure and task complexity while maintaining an embodied and general-data mixture~\cite{zhang2026qwen_2606_17030}. Its five-layer action descriptions provide a common semantic interface across human, robot, and simulated motion. Low-scoring descriptions are routed to prompt revision and human review. Language makes the motion searchable and conditionable, but does not recover calibrated joint control or establish contact validity. The appropriate use is determined by training role. A clip that is useful for video prediction or planning may still be unsuitable for behavior cloning.

\subsection{Validation and Disclosure Bound the Evidence}

No public recipe closes every validation gap. Qwen-RobotManip reports data audits and component ablations. Gemini Robotics reports Motion Transfer controls and both simulation and real-robot evaluations, while noting that simulation supports most development iteration~\cite{team2025gemini_2510_03342}. GR00T reports generated-data and retention studies. The $\pi$ family reports mixture, metadata, and rollout ablations. Qwen-RobotWorld reports caption screening and generation evaluations. These are complementary forms of evidence, not entries in one leaderboard.

The reports also differ in what can be reproduced. Data access, preparation code, scoring checkpoints, source weights, model weights, and evaluation assets should be recorded separately. A system can disclose its model and still leave the causal path from raw records to training units unavailable. Across these systems, the stable pattern is not a specific mixture ratio. It is the separation of source roles, explicit alignment, checks matched to the intended supervision, stage-specific routing, and evaluation that can revise later decisions.
