\section{Industrial Data Recipes and Self-Evolving Data Engines}
\label{sec:recipes-engines}

The preceding sections isolate preparation operations.  Embodied foundation
models, however, are built as recipes: several source types, alignments,
annotations, filters, training roles, and feedback loops change together.  These
systems are scientifically important because they reveal which data operations
survive at industrial scale, but they create a distinct evidence problem.  A
technical report may establish the behavior of the complete model while leaving
source weights, rejected-data statistics, labor, or intermediate checkpoints
undisclosed.  We therefore use reports as \emph{system evidence}, not as license
to assign an end-to-end gain to one hidden preparation step.

Table~\ref{tab:industrial-recipes} reconstructs five public recipe families from
primary reports.  We record only disclosed quantities and mark our synthesis as
such.  Comparisons are structural rather than numerical: an hour of egocentric
video, an hour of a 50-Hz robot trajectory, a simulated rollout, and a
video--text clip are not equivalent units; neither are evaluations on different
robots, tasks, model sizes, or budgets.

\subsection{What the Industrial Recipes Actually Reveal}

\textbf{Alignment is a precondition for scale, not cleanup after scale.}
Qwen-RobotManip makes this principle unusually explicit.  Its approximately
38.1k-hour corpus combines more than 11k hours of robot demonstrations, 1,933
hours of egocentric human video, and 24,808 hours of human-to-robot synthesis
across 15 morphologies~\cite{yuan2026qwen_2606_17846}.  The scale is enabled by
a chain that first retargets and smooths hand motion, removes hands and
reconstructs the background, searches for a feasible robot base and inverse-
kinematic solution, and depth-composites the robot.  It then applies five
state--action checks and three cross-modal checks.  This is materially different
from concatenating datasets and zero-padding actions: a masked 80-D schema fixes
field meaning, camera-frame end-effector deltas and camera positional encoding
align motion, and prompts expose embodiment and control mode to the learner.
The report's audit found severe source-specific defects---including a subset in
which its state--action trend test rejected 81\% of episodes---which is direct
evidence that ``published robot data'' is not synonymous with ``training-ready
robot data.''

The same conclusion appears in a different form in Gemini Robotics 1.5.  Merely
adding other embodiments generally helps, but the reported single-embodiment,
multi-embodiment-without-Motion-Transfer, and full-recipe ablation shows that a
motion-alignment mechanism changes how much cross-robot data can be used
~\cite{team2025gemini_2510_03342}.  The benefit is asymmetric: data from other
robots matters most for a low-data target, whereas a well-covered ALOHA platform
has less to gain.  Thus, ``cross-embodiment scale'' should be reported as a
source--target transfer matrix, not a global hour count.

\textbf{Training role is often more informative than source type.}
$\pi_{0.5}$ uses mobile-manipulator, static multi-environment,
cross-embodiment laboratory, high-level, verbal-instruction, and web data for
different purposes.  Its broad pretraining mixture is mostly not the exact
target household mobile-manipulation distribution; post-training then routes
short successful episodes while retaining web/high-level supervision and adding
verbal-instruction demonstrations~\cite{intelligence20250_2504_16054}.  An
ingredient can consequently be low value as an action target but high value as
a semantic anchor or task decomposer.

$\pi_{0.7}$ pushes role separation further.  Instead of discarding every
failure or suboptimal rollout, it annotates speed, quality, mistakes, strategy,
and control mode and conditions the policy on the desired metadata
~\cite{intelligence20260_2604_15483}.  In its ablations, autonomous evaluation
data and metadata both matter; without contextual metadata, adding progressively
lower-quality data can hurt, whereas the conditioned model continues to benefit
in the reported setting.  This resolves an apparent conflict in the curation
literature.  ``Keep only successes'' is reasonable when all retained actions
are interpreted as expert targets.  ``Keep failures'' can be reasonable when
outcome, mistake intervals, and desired behavior are represented and the
objective can use them.  The disagreement is about \emph{routing and semantics},
not whether a failure bit has universal positive or negative value.

\textbf{Synthetic data is a multiplier only when anchored and routed.}
GR00T N1 organizes web/human, neural/simulated, and real robot data as a pyramid,
using latent or inverse-dynamics actions where ground-truth control is absent and
real data for embodiment grounding~\cite{nvidia2025gr00t_2503_14734}.  In one
neural post-training experiment it samples real and neural trajectories 1:1;
its simulation branch keeps successful trajectories.  DreamGen similarly
separates four stages: adapt an image-to-video model with teleoperated robot
video, generate task/environment-conditioned video, recover pseudo-actions with
a latent-action or inverse-dynamics model, and post-train the policy on the
resulting neural trajectories~\cite{jang2025dreamgen_2505_12705}.  Its
DreamGenBench instruction/physics diagnostics correlate with downstream policy
success in the reported experiments, useful evidence for model-level routing but
not certification that every generated trajectory is executable.  The N1 report
also supplies a valuable negative result: post-training without
left-to-right handover examples removed that capability.  Broad anchors and
minimum mixture floors are therefore not conservative bookkeeping; they are
controls against stage-induced forgetting.

Qwen-RobotWorld expands the generator side with an 8.6M-clip, 200M-plus-frame
corpus spanning more than 20 embodiments and 500 action categories
~\cite{zhang2026qwen_2606_17030}.  Its processing chain first standardizes video,
then produces five-layer action descriptions and sends low-scoring captions from
an LLM judge to targeted prompt revision and human review.  Training preserves a
70:30 embodied/general mixture and progressively introduces single-view,
multi-view, concatenated-view, and complex cross-domain data.  The report also
excludes AI-generated media from its general-data anchor because of artifact and
bias concerns---a useful counterexample to indiscriminate synthetic scaling.
Language can make human, robot, and simulated motion addressable through one
interface, but it does not recover calibrated joint actions or certify contact.
Outputs should therefore be routed by training role: broad video dynamics,
proposal, and planning data need different gates from behavior-cloning targets.
A world model may support virtual evaluation, but the same checkpoint should not
generate and uncritically approve its own samples.

\textbf{Disclosure itself is a recipe property.}
Gemini Robotics reports thousands of hours of ALOHA~2 demonstrations, non-action
multimodal data, and 2k--5k high-quality episodes per specialization task
~\cite{team2025gemini_2503_20020}; version 1.5 reports thousands of tasks over
three embodiments and interleaved real-robot comparisons.  Its Motion Transfer
ablation is unusually diagnostic, while its exact source quantities, filters,
and annotation lineage remain closed.  The same report notes that more than
90\% of development evaluation episodes ran in simulation and that final model
quality still requires real testing~\cite{team2025gemini_2510_03342}.  This is
good systems practice, but it also means simulated iteration speed and real
deployment evidence must remain separate fields.  Reproducibility should thus
be graded component-wise---raw data, transformation code, mixture, model,
verifier, and evaluation---rather than labeling an entire industrial system
simply ``open'' or ``closed.''

\subsection{When a Pipeline Becomes a Data Engine}

A static pipeline computes $\traindata=\pipeline(\rawdata)$ once.  We reserve
\emph{data engine} for a system in which evidence from the current learner
causally changes a future data decision:
\begin{align}
\theta_t &= \operatorname{Train}(\pipeline_t(\mathcal{D}_{\leq t})),\\
E_t &= \operatorname{Audit}(\theta_t;\tau_t),\\
(\pipeline_{t+1},q_{t+1})
 &= \operatorname{Control}(\pipeline_t,E_t,\mathcal{B}_t),
\end{align}
where $E_t$ can contain success, failure, intervention, uncertainty, safety,
coverage, and subgroup outcomes; $q_{t+1}$ requests collection, generation,
relabeling, reweighting, or a verification experiment; and $\mathcal{B}_t$
records human, compute, simulation, and robot budgets.  Re-running the same ETL
script or periodically fine-tuning on all new logs is lifecycle automation, but
not yet a data engine under this definition.

Table~\ref{tab:data-engines} compares representative loops through five
controller fields.  This exposes differences hidden by the common ``flywheel''
label.  RoboCat and AutoRT close acquisition loops through practice and
foundation-model task proposal~\cite{bousmalis2023robocat_2306_11706,ahn2024autort_2401_12963}.
DexFlyWheel is a simulation-centered generative loop seeded by one human
demonstration~\cite{zhu2025dexflywheel_2509_23829}.  RECAP, SOP, and LWD are
post-training loops, but their data semantics and update cadence differ.
Robot-Powered Data Flywheels uses deployment to adapt an upstream perception
model, showing that the updated learner need not be the action policy
~\cite{grannen2025robot_2511_19647}.  The term names a systems pattern, not one
algorithm.

\subsection{RL, Post-Training, and Failure-Driven Evolution}

Post-training changes preparation because the deployed policy determines which
states are observed.  Offline demonstration data approximate an expert or
collector distribution; on-policy data reveal the current model's induced error
distribution.  Their value cannot be compared by hours alone.  SOP reports that,
for one base model and task family, three hours of targeted on-policy interaction
closed substantially more of the gap than an additional 80 hours of static
demonstrations~\cite{pan2026sop_2601_03044}.  This is a within-paper finding, not
a universal exchange rate.  It supports the narrower causal claim that
failure-targeted experience can have high marginal value near deployment.

The key operation is not ``add failures,'' but convert a rollout into typed
training units.  A successful segment may be an imitation target; a valid prefix
of a failed episode can train reaching or value prediction; an intervention
provides both a takeover boundary and a corrective action; a near miss can be a
reward-model negative; an unsafe action can calibrate a safety verifier but must
not silently enter behavior cloning.  Ambiguous cases should be retained with
uncertainty and routed to review.  RoboReward makes this distinction concrete:
it augments success-heavy robot data with counterfactual relabeling and temporal
clipping to construct negatives and near misses for a specialized reward VLM
~\cite{lee2026roboreward_2601_00675}.  Its real-robot study also cautions that a
general embodied-reasoning model is not automatically a calibrated reward model
for the target data distribution.

RECAP combines demonstrations, autonomous rollouts, terminal outcomes, and
human corrections; its value model predicts time to success or a large failure
penalty, and an advantage-conditioned policy extracts improving actions
~\cite{intelligence20250_2511_14759}.  Notably, the reported multi-round
implementation fine-tunes each value/policy pair from the common pretrained
checkpoint instead of the previous iteration, which the authors found useful
for limiting drift.  SOP reduces the latency between failure and correction with
distributed actors, balances tasks uniformly, clips each task's online fraction
to $[0.2,0.8]$, and updates robot checkpoints only between episodes
~\cite{pan2026sop_2601_03044}.  LWD instead retains heterogeneous returns with a
distributional critic and uses mixed offline/online replay; during online
updates, it freezes the VLM backbone and changes the action expert, another
explicit stability choice~\cite{wang2026learning_2605_00416}.  These mechanisms
show that \emph{data evolution and parameter evolution must be co-designed}:
buffer composition, reward semantics, update latency, and trainable modules all
determine whether a failure is useful.

Three evaluations are still rare.  First, a multi-round control must report the
area under the utility--robot-cost curve, not only its final checkpoint.  Second,
it must audit retained broad and safety capabilities, since correcting today's
failure can erase yesterday's rare skill.  Third, it needs a counterfactual
promotion test: compare the proposed new data with a volume- and compute-matched
sample from the same collection round.  Without that control, a better successor
may reflect more interaction, more updates, or a changed reward rather than the
engine's data decision.

\subsection{From Static Curation to a Data-Preparation Agent}

Figure~\ref{fig:autonomy-ladder} organizes the evolution by \emph{decision
authority}.  At L0, people choose a fixed pipeline.  L1 optimizes an offline
subset or mixture for a learner.  L2 uses rollout failures or interventions for
one or a few post-training rounds.  An L3 engine repeatedly executes a
predesigned collect--verify--route--train--deploy controller.  An L4
data-preparation agent must additionally choose which operator or experiment to
run, estimate its information value and cost, decide when evidence is sufficient,
and stop or roll back under risk.  Existing systems demonstrate substantial
pieces through L3; calling them fully autonomous L4 agents would overstate the
evidence.

\input{figures/tex/autonomy_ladder}

Formally, an agent observes a versioned state
\begin{equation}
h_t=(\mathcal{D}_{\leq t},\theta_t,\mathcal{V}_t,\mathcal{C}_t,
\mathcal{B}_t,\mathcal{R}_t),
\end{equation}
containing data/model lineage, verifier calibration, coverage gaps, remaining
budget, and safety risk.  It chooses $u_t$ from a typed operator set
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}=\{&\text{acquire, repair, label, segment, filter, route, mix,}\\
              &\text{generate, train, audit, rollback, stop}\},\qquad
u_t\in\mathcal{A},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and receives intrinsic diagnostics, proxy scores, controlled training outcomes,
or rollouts at different delays and costs.  The objective is long-horizon,
constrained utility,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\pi_{\phi}}\quad&
\mathbb{E}\sum_t\gamma^t[\Delta\utility_t-\lambda C_t-\nu R_t],\\
\text{s.t.}\quad&\text{safety and anchor-retention constraints}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
not immediate improvement on a mutable judge.

This formulation creates four requirements beyond an automated data engine.
The agent needs a typed and reversible operator library; calibrated uncertainty
over delayed policy utility; causal experiment design that changes one data
decision at a time; and governance that can abstain, enforce immutable anchors,
canary-train, and roll back data and model versions together.  An LLM can serve
as planner or semantic interface, but language fluency does not supply physical
validation or permission to conduct risky exploration.  The route from L3 to L4
is therefore not simply ``add an LLM.''  It is the construction of an auditable
experimental controller whose autonomy grows only as its evidence and safety
envelope grow.
