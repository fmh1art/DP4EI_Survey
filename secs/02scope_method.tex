\section{Scope and Review Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

We conduct a systematic \emph{scoping} review rather than a statistical meta-analysis.
The field does not share a common data intervention, learner, dataset, or outcome protocol.
Combining success-rate differences across papers would therefore produce a numerical estimate from experiments that are not comparable.
Our goals are to map the design space, record common method properties, assess the strength of each type of evidence, and derive hypotheses for future controlled studies.

\subsection{Research Questions and Eligibility}

Four research questions govern the review.
RQ1 asks which decisions turn raw embodied records into training examples or change how those examples are sampled.
RQ2 asks which signals guide those decisions, at what data granularity, and under which assumptions.
RQ3 asks how a paper validates data value and when its results can be compared with other work.
RQ4 asks how training or deployment experience changes the next round of data collection or preparation.

A work is eligible when it describes an identifiable operation on embodied data, a source design that changes downstream compatibility or coverage, or an empirical analysis of data composition.
Eligible sources include peer-reviewed conference and journal papers, arXiv papers, dataset and benchmark papers, and technical reports with enough methodological detail to recover the data operation.
Official project and repository documentation is used only for released formats, artifacts, and implementation facts.
A work is excluded from the core synthesis if it only consumes an existing dataset, changes only the policy architecture, or presents a generic simulator without a data transformation.
We also exclude navigation and driving work without a transferable preparation mechanism and records without enough primary material to identify the operation and supporting evidence.

\subsection{Discovery and Screening}

The search covers January 2018 through August 2, 2026.
The baseline search combines robot, embodied, or VLA with terms for data curation, demonstration selection, quality, filtering, mixture design, relabeling, segmentation, augmentation, synthetic demonstrations, data engines, self-improvement, and cross-embodiment learning.
We run these queries in DBLP and arXiv.
We then trace citations from central papers and check major datasets, benchmarks, laboratories, industrial reports, project pages, and public data documentation.
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} provides the exact strings, raw database responses, query-level errors, and the script used to rerun the search.

The resolved snapshot contains 1,160 title-deduplicated records returned by 28 queries.
Eight queries failed transiently in the initial run and were retried successfully.
We retain both the original errors and the resolved responses.
The 1,160 records form the candidate pool and should not be interpreted as the final number of included works.

On August 2, we ran a separate targeted search for industrial data recipes, online and fleet post-training, robot data flywheels, robot reward models, and large language model (LLM) instruction selection and self-training risks.
We verified exact titles, checked official laboratory reports, and traced citations from the primary sources.
This pass promoted five existing records to deep reading and added seven eligible works.
We preserve the targeted search as a separate record rather than adding its results to the earlier 1,160-record count.

Title and abstract screening removes uses of ``data curation'' and ``data engine'' that are unrelated to the scope of this survey.
Full-text screening then requires a recoverable preparation operation, decision signal, or finding about data composition.
Duplicate arXiv and published versions are merged as one work.
We cite the published version when its metadata and content can be verified and otherwise cite the arXiv version.
Updated technical reports replace earlier reports, while scale claims that depend on a particular version retain the date of that version.
Citation tracing stops when another pass produces no new eligible mechanism or major system.
Papers discovered after the cutoff are logged for the next update rather than silently inserted into the current corpus.

The resulting evidence map contains 211 unique works.
For 68 deep reads, we checked the method and data sections, relevant experiments, and stated limitations.
A further 109 method-level reads cover the abstract and the sections of the primary text that directly describe the preparation method or evidence.
The remaining 34 works are retained as abstract-index records for tracking emerging work and possible omissions.
Only the 177 deep and method-level reads support the technical synthesis.
No technical claim in this paper relies on an abstract-index record.
This distinction prevents the corpus size from implying a depth of evidence that the review did not obtain.

\subsection{Structured Coding and Evidence Strength}

Each core work is coded by its problem, data source, modality, preparation operation, transformed object, granularity, decision signal, and optimization objective.
The coding form also records human involvement, scale, cost, evaluation protocol, generalization, assumptions, failure modes, openness, provenance, publication type, and reading depth.
For a composite system, each material operation receives a separate code.
A system that labels, filters, and synthesizes data therefore receives three operation codes rather than an ``other'' label.
We record the authors' original terminology before normalizing it.
For example, ``selection,'' ``pruning,'' and ``filtering'' can be mapped to a shared operation without assuming that the papers optimize the same objective.

We distinguish four levels of evidence because a change in a data property is not the same as an improvement in a trained robot policy.
\emph{Intrinsic evidence} measures the record itself through properties such as missingness, duplication, smoothness, or labeled noise.
\emph{Proxy evidence} uses an estimator such as representation distance, information, a foundation-model score, a physical check, an influence estimate, or predicted executability.
\emph{Downstream evidence} retrains a policy under a controlled budget and evaluates it in simulation or on a robot.
Evidence is stronger when the downstream effect is reproduced across tasks, learners, data budgets, or embodiments.
These levels instantiate the utility-evidence axis introduced in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}.

Peer-reviewed experiments and reproducible primary papers provide stronger support than unreviewed preprints for the same claim.
Technical reports are valuable sources for engineering recipes, but their internally selected tasks, models, and budgets limit causal conclusions.
Project pages establish release and implementation facts rather than performance effects.
We explicitly label an inference when it combines results across papers instead of restating an author's conclusion.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}

Three limitations remain.
First, industrial preparation pipelines are often under-documented, so public evidence over-represents operations that can be isolated in papers.
Second, terminology is unstable because relevant work may be described as data engines, post-training, imitation learning, models of environment dynamics, or self-improvement.
Multiple queries and citation tracing reduce but cannot eliminate the risk of omission.
Third, most of the newest 2026 works are preprints and cannot provide the same evidence strength as mature peer-reviewed results.
We respond by avoiding a cross-paper ranking, reporting negative or non-monotonic outcomes when available, and publishing the corpus manifest so that omissions and coding decisions can be audited.
