\section{Constructing Training Units: Semantic Enrichment, Curation, and Routing}
\label{sec:curation}

This section covers the second part of the operation axis: turning usable records into training units and assigning those units a role.
After technical alignment, a pipeline must identify the behavior contained in a record and decide how that behavior should be used for training.
These decisions affect each other.
Segmentation determines which unit receives a label or score, while a progress or outcome signal can reveal where a subtask, retry, or recovery begins.

\subsection{Why Episodes Are Too Coarse}

A teleoperated episode can include approach, grasp, transport, failed insertion, retry, recovery, and terminal idle time.
One episode-level success bit maps all transitions to the same target quality despite their different roles.
It also prevents a long trajectory from being recomposed for a new instruction.
Modern preparation increasingly operates on subtask segments and state transitions.

Boundary signals fall into three groups: measured motion or contact, learned semantic change, and explicit operator intervention.
Purely kinematic boundaries are cheap but can split semantically atomic behavior or merge visually quiet planning with idle time.
Foundation-model labeling provides task semantics.
Natural language Instruction Labeling for Scalability (NILS) detects state changes and uses pretrained models to produce language supervision without task-specific training~\cite{blank2024scaling_2410_17772}.
Earlier, Data-driven Instruction Augmentation for Language-conditioned Control (DIAL) propagated a small set of human trajectory descriptions through a vision--language model (VLM) to augment language supervision for a much larger robot dataset ~\cite{xiao2022robotic_2211_11736}.
Their strength is scalable relabeling, while their risk is a correlated error chain in object detection, state-change inference, locating events in time, and language generation.

A robust segmentation record should retain the confidence of each proposed boundary and the signals that produced it.
It should also preserve a hierarchy from episode to phase, primitive, and transition.
Training can then vary an uncertain boundary within a recorded tolerance instead of treating one predicted frame as exact.
This representation supports operations that are unavailable at episode level.
A pipeline can remove only an idle or corrupted interval, attach different language to separate subtasks, or use a failure prefix as context while assigning the recovery suffix to a recovery objective.

\subsection{Foundation-Model Labels Need Their Own Quality Control}

Visual-language models can infer objects, task descriptions, progress, and success at a scale where exhaustive human annotation is impossible.
Their labels are nevertheless proxy evidence, not ground truth.
Their errors usually arise from missed visual evidence, temporal misalignment, or a mismatch between intended and achieved change.
Pretraining bias can make these errors systematic across environments and objects.

Auto-labeling should allow the model to abstain when it is uncertain.
Structured descriptions of the entities, state change, behavior, and outcome should be generated before free text.
Multiple views and temporal windows can expose inconsistent labels.
Cross-field checks can test whether an asserted placement corresponds to a changed object relation.
Confidence should be calibrated on a human audit sampled across tasks, sources, and confidence levels.
Unresolved cases should be routed to review.
Agreement among several prompts of the same model is not independent evidence.
Stronger audits use different model families and compare labels with measurable state or execution where available.

Label utility must also be tested downstream.
A fluent detailed description may increase superficial linguistic variety while fragmenting equivalent tasks into inconsistent instructions.
Conversely, a coarse label can improve sharing but erase goal parameters needed for precise control.
The appropriate granularity depends on the training objective and stage.
A label hierarchy can preserve both coarse sharing and task-specific detail.

\subsection{Failure Is a Type, Not a Quality Level}

Discarding all failed episodes removes unsafe final actions but also removes near-success states, recovery examples, causal information about task boundaries, and precisely the distribution encountered by an imperfect policy.
Keeping them as expert demonstrations is equally problematic.
The useful distinction is structural.
A pipeline should label the cause and onset of a failure, separate valid prefixes and recoveries, mark operator takeover and retry, and route each part to a compatible objective.

Self-Supervised Data Filtering (SSDF) uses relations between expert and imperfect data to select useful segments from failures.
Its simulation and Franka experiments show that imperfect data need not be discarded wholesale~\cite{wu2024learning_2401_08957}.
ReTVL uses retry structure to learn local value rather than assuming monotonic progress ~\cite{qin2026beyond_2606_24633}.
Demo-SCORE goes one step closer to the target policy: a classifier trained on successful and unsuccessful online rollouts filters unreliable demonstration strategies~\cite{chen2025curating_2503_03707}.
These methods resolve an apparent conflict in the literature.  ``Removing failed data helps'' and ``failed data improves robustness'' can both hold because the first often means removing mislabeled action targets, whereas the second uses failure with outcome, boundary, retry, or recovery structure.

\subsection{Signals for Selection and Weighting}

Rules and simple statistics are inexpensive and interpretable.
These signals cover known technical defects, repeated or idle behavior, and coarse outcome labels.
They work well when the defect is known, but a smooth trajectory may be confidently wrong and a corrective recovery may be intentionally non-smooth.
Thresholds also correlate with episode length and task difficulty.

Representation and information measures support richer selection.
Embedding distance can identify duplicates and coverage gaps.
Mutual information between state and action favors demonstrations that are both informative and predictable.
The mutual-information curation study evaluates this signal on simulation and real robot data ~\cite{hejna2025robot_2502_08623}.
Crucially, its difficult Pen-in-Cup setting slightly favors the full dataset over the filtered subset, consistent with lost state coverage.
This negative case rules out a universal ``less is more'' conclusion.
The effect of filtering depends on the accuracy of the estimator, the unit being filtered, and the coverage that remains.
Smoothness-driven RINSE similarly favors smooth trajectories, but the signal should carry less weight in tasks that contain impacts, retries, or rapid corrections~\cite{kulkarni2026learning_2604_23000}.
Dataset-level entropy and learnability analyses broaden the diagnostic view ~\cite{xiao2025data_2511_09119}, while structure-aware selection such as SIEVE operates over motion primitives and their transitions~\cite{wu2026sieve_2607_06442}.
These signals identify records that deserve inspection or selection.
They do not establish that the selected data improve a policy until a controlled training comparison verifies the effect.

Learned progress provides another decision signal.
SCIZOR learns a task-progress signal and deduplicates in joint state--action space, moving beyond image-only similarity ~\cite{zhang2025scizor_2505_22626}.
This is attractive at scale, but progress is not monotonic in assembly, search, recovery, or tasks that temporarily undo a state.
Progress-based filters require retry-aware calibration and should retain low-confidence non-monotonic segments for targeted evaluation.

Learner-aware methods ask how a training unit affects a particular learner or target set.
Influence estimates approximate how changing the weight or membership of a unit changes loss on that target.
DataMIL estimates data effects from subset-trained models~\cite{dass2025datamil_2505_09603}.
QoQ defines quality through reduction of loss on validation demonstrations and aggregates transition influence within trajectories~\cite{lee2026quality_2603_09056}.
ATHENA scales heterogeneous influence approximations to multi-task VLA curation and evaluates both benchmark and real-robot tasks ~\cite{xu2026athena_2606_16208}.
These methods more directly approximate Equation~\ref{eq:conditional-value}, but their estimates are tied to the chosen target.
A narrow or biased validation set makes its preferences the definition of quality.
Approximations used to avoid full retraining can be unstable, and repeated training, rollouts, or influence computation can dominate the saved training cost.

\subsection{Detection Quality Is Not Policy Utility}

The controlled audit ``What Demonstration Curation Metrics Do to Your Policy'' explicitly separates detection of injected defects from the performance of a policy trained after curation~\cite{bedi2026what_2606_10229}.
It finds that a metric's ability to recognize an intended structural defect can disagree with downstream utility and exposes episode-length confounding.
Its small task and rollout scale limit generality, but the methodological warning applies broadly.
A detector can achieve a high area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) by identifying a visible artifact while removing rare states needed for recovery.
Policy utility can improve even with modest defect recall if the removed examples exert disproportionate gradients.

A curation study should first establish whether the operation changed its intended data property and what else changed at the chosen threshold.
It should then test whether that intervention improves a trained policy under equal data access and compute, reporting the target distribution and uncertainty.
The first question diagnoses the mechanism.
Only the second supports a utility claim.

\subsection{Mixtures, Sampling, and Role Routing}

Cross-source training adds a joint decision.
Proportions affect both gradient scale and how often rare capabilities are seen.
Uniform sampling overweights large sources.
Uniform-by-dataset can over-amplify tiny noisy sources, while fixed hand weights become stale as the learner changes.
Re-Mix learns mixture weights with an objective that protects the worst-performing data group and reports large gains over uniform or human mixtures in its own two-embodiment evaluation ~\cite{hejna2024re_2408_14037}.
The paper also identifies a revealing failure: the learned objective can increase weight on abnormal action distributions.
The optimization target requires constraints and independent audits.

Language-model preparation offers transferable mechanisms, not transferable quality definitions.
DoReMi learns domain weights from the relative loss of a small proxy model.
Data Selection for Language Models via Importance Resampling (DSIR) instead resamples examples according to an importance estimate for the target distribution~\cite{xie2023doremi_2305_10429,xie2023data_2302_03169}.
In robotics, analogous mixture updates must account for temporal correlation, embodiment, execution cost, and physical validity.
A useful hierarchy is source $\rightarrow$ task $\rightarrow$ episode $\rightarrow$ segment, with minimum sampling shares that protect rare embodiments and safety-critical failures from collapse.

Routing changes the purpose of a record rather than only its sampling frequency.
A pipeline can use broad but weakly aligned video for representation pretraining and reserve aligned robot actions for behavior cloning, which trains a policy to reproduce recorded actions.
Unsuccessful attempts with outcome labels can support value or recovery learning, while uncertain synthetic episodes can train a verifier.
Stage-conditioned routing explains why industrial recipes often use a broad pretraining mixture followed by narrower embodiment and task post-training, and why one fixed mixture is rarely optimal for the entire lifecycle.

\subsection{Synthesis: The Correctness--Coverage--Cost Trade-off}

Across curation methods, a stricter threshold often improves one measured property while reducing coverage and data volume.
Greater diversity can expand the range of states but may also introduce incompatible action conventions.
Smoothness checks can reject noise and corrective behavior at the same time.
A learner-aware score can improve short-term validation loss while narrowing transfer to other tasks or models.
No single threshold optimizes correctness, coverage, target alignment, and cost in every setting.
A robust system should report these separate effects, periodically compare inexpensive scores with fixed-budget policy training, and retain rejected records with reason codes.
These records allow an earlier decision to be reversed when the learner or target changes.
