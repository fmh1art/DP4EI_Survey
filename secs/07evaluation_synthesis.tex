\section{Evaluation and Cross-Cutting Synthesis}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The literature contains many positive within-paper results but few fair cross-paper comparisons.
Robots, tasks, data pools, policy families, model initializations, training steps, extra pretraining data, and success definitions usually differ.
A number reported in one setting establishes that an operation can work under that setting.
It does not rank the operation against a number from another paper.
This section specifies the evidence needed for comparison and then synthesizes conclusions that recur despite protocol differences.

\subsection{Three Levels of Outcome}

Intrinsic outcomes describe properties of the prepared data, such as invalid-file rate, label-noise estimate, duplicate rate, smoothness, entropy, coverage, balance, or retained fraction.
They are cheap, interpretable diagnostics.
They do not show that the measured property is causally useful to a policy.

Proxy outcomes include foundation-model scores, information estimates, influence, predicted executability, inverse-kinematics feasibility, simulator replay, or a learned success classifier.
They are closer to a target, but their calibration set, model bias, and chosen decision threshold must be reported.
The same proxy should not define both the filter and the test of filtering.

Downstream outcomes retrain a policy and measure success, generalization, sample efficiency, recovery, and safety.
This is the strongest evidence for training utility and the most expensive.
Simulation affords repetitions and control over task factors.
Real execution tests physical validity but often permits only a few trials.
The two should be reported separately, with means, uncertainty, and trial counts.
Evidence is stronger when an effect persists across learners, data budgets, embodiments, and held-out task factors.

\subsection{Controlling the Intervention}

Table~\ref{tab:evaluation-protocol} gives a minimum protocol.
Its central design is a controlled comparison among the unprepared full data, a random subset matched to the retained volume, the proposed prepared subset, and, where relevant, an oracle using ground-truth defect or utility labels.
A volume-matched random control separates selection from effects caused by using less data or fewer updates.
The full-data control tests whether information was unnecessarily removed.
The oracle quantifies how much headroom is due to the metric versus the underlying intervention.

Compute must be controlled in two complementary regimes.
Under \emph{fixed-update} evaluation, every policy receives the same number of gradient steps and batch samples, measuring value per training unit.
Under \emph{fixed-pass} evaluation, each sees the same number of passes through its own data, measuring an end-to-end recipe.
Reporting both reveals whether a smaller curated set helps because its units are more valuable or because it is revisited more often.
Hyperparameter tuning budgets must also match: a method-specific threshold tuned on the test tasks is extra supervision.

Data leakage is subtle in embodied settings.
Near-identical scene configurations can occur in different episodes.
Generated variants can cross a split, a VLM labeler may have seen benchmark images, and target demonstrations used for influence can overlap evaluation objects.
Splits should occur before augmentation and at the highest shared grouping available, such as task instance, object, scene, collection session, or operator.
Lineage-based duplicate checks should then verify the separation.

\subsection{Cost, Openness, and Reproducibility}

``Using 50\% of the data'' is not a cost result if choosing that half required training many policies, running target-robot rollouts, or manually labeling every episode.
Report preparation cost in human minutes, foundation-model calls, accelerator hours, simulator rollouts, real-robot hours, storage and input/output operations, and safety interventions.
Then report total cost to reach a target deployment utility.
Expensive learner-aware selection can still be worthwhile when its preparation cost is shared across many long pretraining runs.
A cheap rule can be preferable for a small adaptation run.

Reproducibility requires more than releasing final data.
The operator code, model checkpoint used for scoring, prompt and threshold, source version, exclusion log, learned weights, random seeds, and lineage should be available.
OpenVLA and Octo make policy baselines more accessible ~\cite{kim2024openvla_2406_09246,team2024octo_2405_12213}, but reproducible preparation additionally needs a raw or defect-injected pool on which alternative operators can be rerun.
Closed industrial reports can reveal scale and recipes, yet claims depending on unavailable data or internal rollouts remain weaker evidence for causal comparison.

\subsection{What Transfers from Language and Multimodal Data Engineering}

The most useful lesson from language-model data work is methodological: treat the data pipeline itself as an optimizable, versioned system.
RefinedWeb and FineWeb expose staged filtering and deduplication.
DataComp-LM fixes a 240-trillion-token candidate pool, model and compute tracks, and 53 evaluations so that preparation can be compared under a controlled training intervention ~\cite{penedo2023refinedweb_2306_01116,penedo2024fineweb_2406_17557,li2024datacomp_2406_11794}.
DoReMi and DSIR learn mixture or resampling decisions instead of treating source ratios as invisible constants~\cite{xie2023doremi_2305_10429,xie2023data_2302_03169}.
LESS estimates how training examples affect targeted instruction tuning, while the Data-Efficient Instruction Tuning for Alignment method (DEITA) separates quality, complexity, and diversity rather than selecting on one score~\cite{xia2024less_2402_04333,liu2023what_2312_15685}.
The joint-example selection method JEST further shows that the useful object can be a jointly selected batch, not an independently ranked example~\cite{evans2024data_2406_17711}.

Pretraining studies show the value of controlled pipeline competition.
The DataComp-LM design is more informative for embodied intelligence than any individual text filter.
It answers a causal question: under a fixed raw pool, learner, token budget, and evaluation suite, which preparation pipeline produces the best model?
Robot papers often instead compare a curated set and a baseline with different episode counts, updates, augmentations, or policy architectures.
A controlled robot-data benchmark must hold the raw pool, learner, budget, and evaluation fixed while adding action semantics, episode- and segment-level splits, separate simulation and real-robot evaluation, preparation cost, and safety.
Unlike text tokens, robot transitions are not freely exchangeable, and their acquisition price depends on resets, supervision, embodiment, and risk.

Mixture optimization also suggests hierarchical, stage-specific routing.
Language corpora have domains.
Embodied corpora have nested source, task, environment, skill, embodiment, episode, segment, and transition groups.
A single learned source weight captures only the top of the routing hierarchy.
A useful transfer of DoReMi and DSIR is to train a cheap proxy learner and estimate which groups reduce loss on the target distribution, then test whether the learned weights transfer to a larger VLA.
The target distribution cannot be assumed to remain fixed.
Once a robot policy is deployed, its failures change and its data distribution follows.
A ratio learned offline becomes an initial setting for later controller updates, not a permanent optimum.
Minimum sampling shares are also needed for rare safety and recovery groups even when their short-term average loss contribution is small.

Instruction-tuning research suggests that selection should be tailored to the target learner and task rather than produce one universal ranking.
LESS and DEITA also keep correctness, difficulty, and diversity as separate considerations.
For embodied intelligence, the unit being selected is often a coherent skill segment, correction, or failure--recovery pair rather than a whole episode.
Gradient influence is an informative proxy, but a score for one transition can miss three dependencies.
It can miss which earlier actions caused a later outcome, which approach and grasp segments are useful only together, and how an improved policy changes the states visited later.
The relevant test is whether the selected set improves a policy under a fixed budget on held-out tasks and learners.
A high selection score alone does not provide that evidence.

LLM post-training suggests iterative judges, but it also exposes their failure mode.
Self-Rewarding Language Models illustrate a loop in which a model generates candidates, judges responses, constructs preference pairs, and updates itself~\cite{yuan2024self_2401_10020}.
In an embodied system, the same mechanism could generate alternative failure examples, score trajectories with a reward VLM, collect new behavior, and repeat.
The risks are more severe when actions affect the physical world.
A VLM may accept a physically impossible contact, and a policy can change the visual distribution on which its judge was calibrated.
Work on recursively generated training data shows the more general risk.
Indiscriminate replacement of natural data can erase rare parts of the distribution across generations~\cite{shumailov2024collapse}.
We do not claim that this language-model result directly predicts robot-policy collapse.
Instead, it motivates a design constraint.
Self-evolving embodied pipelines should retain audited real data, identify synthetic lineage, use independent verifiers, preserve minimum sampling shares for rare groups, and test each round for rare-case and safety retention.

The transferable lessons concern experimental and system mechanisms, not the meaning of a quality score.
Text pipelines teach how to version operators, run controlled comparisons under fixed compute, optimize mixtures, estimate target influence, and audit recursive generation.
They do not supply action causality, timing, controller semantics, physical validity, or safe exploration.
Those missing properties explain why autonomous embodied data preparation requires the layered evidence and control architecture in Section~\ref{sec:recipes-engines}, rather than a direct port of web-data filters or an LLM judge.

\subsection{A Controlled Robot-Data Evaluation Track}

We propose four linked assets for such a benchmark.
The first is a versioned raw pool that spans multiple embodiments and sources.
It should include natural and controlled defects, failed and recovery episodes, redundant coverage, and deliberate near-duplicate or source-overlap cases for testing leakage.
The second is a preparation interface that requires each submission to emit training units, sampling weights, training roles, lineage, and cost logs rather than only a cleaned archive.
The third is a set of fixed lightweight and generalist policy learners trained under several update budgets.
The fourth is an evaluation suite that separates defect detection, held-out simulation, and a smaller real-robot audit on tasks hidden from submitters.
The audit should cover familiar conditions, new combinations of familiar factors, and distribution shifts.
Results should report deployment performance, the weakest task or embodiment group, preparation and training cost, and safety separately instead of collapsing them into one rank.

Stress tests should vary contamination, episode length, label noise, action delay, rare recovery frequency, source imbalance, and embodiment mismatch.
A method that detects one injected artifact but fails under natural mixed defects is not a general quality metric.
Fixed raw snapshots make results repeatable.
Periodically refreshing evaluation cases that remain hidden from submitters can measure overfitting and support continued updates to the survey.

\subsection{Cross-Paper Findings}

Four findings recur despite differences in tasks and evaluation protocols.
First, the value of a record depends on the learner and target task.
Formal analysis, learned mixture weights, subset-trained models, and influence methods all support this conclusion~\cite{belkhale2023data_2306_02437,hejna2024re_2408_14037,dass2025datamil_2505_09603,lee2026quality_2603_09056}.
Some express the dependence directly, while others encode it through a target validation set.
A score learned without evidence from the target setting should therefore be treated as an initial estimate rather than a universal quality measure.
Second, selection must balance correctness against coverage.
Strict filtering can remove harmful labels and rare recovery states at the same time.
The hard-task result for mutual-information filtering, the controlled curation audit, and Re-Mix's abnormal-source failure each show that a plausible score can choose a harmful balance~\cite{hejna2025robot_2502_08623,bedi2026what_2606_10229,hejna2024re_2408_14037}.

Third, useful decisions are increasingly made below the episode level.
Segment recovery, progress-based selection, retry value, and transition influence extract different training roles from one mixed-quality trajectory~\cite{wu2024learning_2401_08957,zhang2025scizor_2505_22626,wu2026sieve_2607_06442,qin2026beyond_2606_24633,lee2026quality_2603_09056}.
This distinction explains much of the apparent disagreement about failed data.
An unmarked failed action sequence is not equivalent to a valid prefix, a marked retry, a recovery, a negative example for a verifier, or a safety boundary.
Each boundary should retain uncertainty, and lineage should link every derived segment to its original episode and training role.

Fourth, transformed data are useful only when the intended supervision remains valid after the transformation.
Common tensor shapes and masks do not align reference frames, timing, controller effects, morphology, or contact across robots.
Likewise, segment recomposition and world-model generation can increase the number of examples while introducing semantic, geometric, kinematic, or physical errors~\cite{mandlekar2023mimicgen_2310_17596,li2026xiaomi_2607_11643}.
These errors require separate checks and ultimately a downstream policy evaluation.

Feedback loops make data and model versions part of one experimental record.
The current model selects experience that trains its successor, so reproduction and rollback require the raw source, preparation decisions, verifier, training role, model version, and deployment evidence.
RECAP, SOP, and LWD use current-policy experience but also retain a pretrained or offline reference or restrict the update path ~\cite{intelligence20250_2511_14759,pan2026sop_2601_03044,wang2026learning_2605_00416}.
Results on recursively generated language data do not prove that robot policies will collapse, but they explain why these controls deserve direct tests ~\cite{shumailov2024collapse}.
Each round needs a stated coverage or failure hypothesis, a matched comparison, retained reference data, and a rollback condition.
Accumulating more self-generated data is not itself evidence of improvement.

\subsection{Paradigm Evolution}

The field's evolution is better understood by asking who chooses each preparation decision than by arranging papers only by year (Figure~\ref{fig:autonomy-ladder}).
Static rule pipelines first made records technically usable.
Pretrained models then supplied scalable semantic labels and verifiers.
Learner-aware selectors then used loss, subset-trained models, influence, and progress to estimate how useful data are for a specific learner and task.
Failure-driven post-training added states visited by the current policy and interventions.
Current data engines repeatedly couple acquisition, verification, routing, and deployment.
The prospective preparation agent would also choose the experiment and know when to abstain, stop, or roll back.
Each step raises target relevance and decision scope, but it also increases cost and the risk that a model reinforces errors in the data it selects.
A stable architecture assigns obvious technical defects to rules and scalable semantic decisions to models.
It uses learner-aware estimates when cheaper signals disagree and reserves simulation or real audits for calibration.
These layers operate inside a versioned controller.
They are not replaced wholesale by one autonomous judge.
